    Who gets called “oknum”?

    On pages about wrongdoing, the word “oknum” (a rogue individual) sits beside teachers, civil servants, soldiers and village heads more often than beside legislators or judges: two to five times in Kompas and Detik. Of 200 “oknum” we read, 150 are not named.

    150 / 200 “oknum” we read are not named

    It is not the police who extort: it is an “oknum” of the police, a rogue individual. Not a teacher who hits a pupil: an “oknum” teacher. One small word, often accused of saving an institution's good name. So look at whom it stands beside most often, in stories about wrongdoing.

    Beside whom?

    Take every 2020–2024 story about wrongdoing that names teachers. What share also contains the word oknum? Then do the same for police, soldiers, judges and the rest. Each row is a group, each dot a publisher.

    The word after “oknum”

    Now turn the question around: on all pages containing oknum, 2011–2024, which word comes right after it? The list of groups is not our pick; these are the words that really do stand there most often.

    What this cannot tell you

    Pages are counted, not sentences. A page counts if it holds a word of wrongdoing (illegal levies, extortion, assault, beating, molestation, rape, fraud, bribery, corruption, drugs, shooting, harassment, and their verb forms) and names a group; the word list is in the question file.

    Sharing a page is not being accused. “Oknum” on a page need not be said of the group named there: police, prosecutors and judges are often on such pages as the ones handling a case, so their figures are too low. The cleaner comparison is teachers, civil servants and soldiers against legislators and doctors.

    A page naming two groups counts for both.

    Of the 200 places we read, 40 are ordinary people with no office (“oknum pasien”, “oknum masyarakat”) and 12 are unclear. Two thirds of that sample is from Tribunnews, because places were drawn in proportion to pages.

    This does not say why. People of low rank are more often left unnamed, and the word “oknum” fills the place of the name.

    The word after “oknum”. For the second figure we first counted, in a third of the text, every word standing right after “oknum”, then grouped the seventy most frequent into seventeen rows and counted them all in the whole text. One page can fall in several rows. At least 15–29% of “oknum” pages, depending on the publisher, fall in no row.
