    The state picks a word. Newsrooms do not always follow

    A presidential decree replaced “Cina” with “Tiongkok”: Kompas went along in 2014–2015 and then turned back, Tempo never did. But the police's term for the armed groups in Papua, “KKB”, led in all three until 2023.

    The state replaced “Cina” with “Tiongkok”: Kompas went along in 2014–2015 and then turned back, Tempo never did

    Who gets to name a thing: the state, or the people who write the news? Three contests between the official word and the newsroom's, with three different results.

    A presidential decree against the style book

    Who names the armed groups in Papua

    Four names, nine publishers

    Of the pages containing the word Papua, what share uses each name? KKB is the police's term. KST, “separatist terrorist group”, is the government's term since 2021. TPNPB is the name the group uses for itself. OPM is its older name. One dot per publisher, including the police's site and the armed forces' site.

    Nineteen coinages: nine lead, ten trail

    Nineteen foreign words, each with the Indonesian word or spelling meant to replace it. Each dot: of every hundred times either one turns up on a Kompas page, how many times is it the Indonesian word? Right of the middle line, the replacement leads.

    What this cannot tell you

    Counting names says nothing about which name is right.

    The yearly figures are coarse. The word “cina” has senses other than the country; “opm” and “kkb” are rare (in 2021, OPM is on about 23 of 180 thousand Kompas pages).

    Winning in Kompas is not winning in the street. The contests between coinages are from one publisher only, Kompas, and a word winning there is a decision of the newsroom's style, not proof that Indonesians use it.

    Names for the armed groups in Papua. The “four names” figure counts pages containing the word “papua”, whatever the story, so the shares are small. For 2024 there are only 105 Papua pages from Tribunnews, too few to count from Okezone and Detik, and the police's site has enough pages only from 2019.

    Which coinages are counted. Nineteen pairs in which both words are written often enough by Kompas and have one meaning only. Left out for having other meanings: tautan (link), laman (website), pemutakhiran (update). Left out because the replacement is not among Kompas's 20 thousand most frequent words: pranala, saltik, takarir, narahubung, peladen, tetikus, siniar, pemengaruh, tengkes, lantatur; counting them would add to the losers. Only base forms are counted (“unggah”, not “mengunggah”). “Leads” means more than half in 2024.

    Not all of these were coined by the national language agency: “hoaks” is an Indonesian spelling of the same word, “karantina wilayah” is the term of a law, and “petahana” was introduced by a newspaper editor.

    The 2014 decree also changed the word for the people to “Tionghoa”; that is not counted here.
