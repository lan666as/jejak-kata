    What the news stopped talking about at each change of term

    After SBY: fuel prices, labour, clashes, and the word “didesak” (was urged). After Jokowi's first term: haze, radicalism, sting operations. The list is made by a rule, and holds only words that shrank in three outlets at once.

    4.3× the fall of “harga bahan bakar” after 2014, in every outlet

    We argue a great deal about what gets reported. We seldom ask what stopped being reported. Here a single rule answers. No word was picked by hand.

    The rule

    Take common words (names of people, bodies and places thrown out) that sat on at least 0.2% of each outlet's pages in the first period. Rank them by how far they fell, using the most cautious figure: the smallest fall among the three outlets, at the low end of its 95% range. So no single outlet can get a word onto the list. Neither can a handful of pages.

    What this cannot tell you

    What is measured is the share of pages, not the number of stories. All three outlets publish far more pages now (collected from Kompas: 111 thousand, then 311 thousand, then 648 thousand), and more of them are not political news. A word's share can shrink while its stories do not.

    The events, or the attention? This cannot tell whether the events became fewer (the haze of 2015 was exceptional) or the newsrooms' attention moved.

    The terms are only dividers. The periods follow presidential terms, but nothing here shows a president as the cause.

    Some words came back: buruh in 2020, harga bahan bakar in 2022.

    A phrase counts as a word of its own. One with a Wikipedia title of its own (“video viral”, “tindak pidana korupsi”) is not counted under the single word.
