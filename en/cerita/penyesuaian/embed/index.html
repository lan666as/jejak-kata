    Did the price rise, or was it “adjusted”?

    “Penyesuaian harga” (price adjustment) is used almost only for prices the state sets: fuel, electricity, tolls, health insurance. Of 200 we read, 139 mean a rise, and 120 are spoken by the government or a state company.

    139 / 200 “price adjustments” we read mean a rise

    Chili goes up. Rice goes up. Petrol? It is “adjusted”. The word has long been suspected of being a euphemism. A suspicion can be tested: for what kind of price is the word used, and who is saying it?

    Two kinds of price

    Two rows: stories about prices the state sets, and stories about market prices. Each dot is a publisher: how often penyesuaian harga or penyesuaian tarif turns up there.

    What this cannot tell you

    Pages are counted, not sentences. A page counts if it names a price the state sets (bbm, pertamax, pertalite, solar, tarif listrik, bpjs, tarif tol, elpiji) or a market price (cabai, bawang, beras, telur, daging, minyak goreng, gula). In it we look for the phrase “penyesuaian harga” or “penyesuaian tarif”, and for “kenaikan”, “naiknya” or “menaikkan” of a price or tariff. A page can name both kinds of price.

    The figures are too low. Other phrasings (“harga disesuaikan”, “menyesuaikan harga”) are not counted.

    Who is speaking is not always clear. In the 200 places we read, the excerpt is often cut; the direction of the price is more certain.

    “Penyesuaian” is used for cuts too: 19 of the 200.

    Toll roads are a different matter. There “penyesuaian tarif” (tariff adjustment) is the law's own term, which sets a review and adjustment every two years. A newsroom using it there is quoting the law, not softening.
