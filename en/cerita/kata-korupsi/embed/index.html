    Which parties get written with the word “korupsi”

    While Demokrat governed, “korupsi” was on 17–25% of the pages naming it. While PDI Perjuangan governed: 11% at most. In 2020–2024 all ten parties that have sat in parliament since 2009 are at 2–10%.

    17–25% of Demokrat's pages carried “korupsi” while it governed

    Which party is the most corrupt? Sorry: this data cannot answer that. What can be counted is simpler: in stories naming a party or an institution, how often are the words korupsi and tersangka (suspect) written too? And what happens to that number when power changes hands?

    The word “korupsi”, party by party

    All ten parties that held seats in the DPR (parliament) between 2009 and 2024, none left out. Each dot is an outlet: of its pages naming the party on the left, what share also contain korupsi? The hollow rings mark 2010–2014.

    Parties outside parliament

    Parties that ran in elections but won no DPR seats in 2009–2024. A dot exists only where the outlet has at least 80 pages naming the party in that period. There are far fewer pages, so the figures are shakier.

    What this cannot tell you

    Sharing a page is not being accused. A party can share a page with “korupsi” because its members became suspects, because it criticised another party, or because both appear in one long story.

    The Attorney General's Office and the Supreme Court are at the top because handling cases is their work.

    This data does not know the true number of cases. Demokrat in 2010–2014 did face large cases that went to trial. Its figure fits that; this data cannot prove more.

    Do not read this as “PDI Perjuangan is cleaner” or “the press is softer on today's rulers”. Both, and also a weaker KPK after 2019 and the shrinking of all corruption news, draw the same picture.

    On spelling. Kompas writes “PDI Perjuangan”; for the spelling “pdip” used by Tempo and Tribunnews the figures are similar (9%, 10%, 8% and 8%, 5%, 5%).

    A phrase counts as a word of its own. One with a Wikipedia title of its own (“video viral”, “tindak pidana korupsi”) is not counted under the single word.

    A case is named for three parties only. Demokrat, PKS and Golkar are the three parties with the highest figures, and a large case is named only at each one’s peak period. The other figures are given no cause. No cause named here does not mean there was no case: other parties had members tried too.

    The seven institutions in the third figure are those in all three outlets' vocabularies, not a complete list of state bodies.

    Which parties are counted. The first figure holds every party with seats in the DPR between 2009 and 2024; the second, the parties without seats that have enough pages. Partai Buruh is left out because its name is also that of the Labour parties of Britain and Australia; Partai Garuda and PKN because they have too few pages.

    Three names mean two things. “demokrat” is also the Democratic Party of the United States: the “demokrat” pages that mention America or its politicians are 2–8% in 2010–2014, 3–13% in 2015–2019 and 5–22% in 2020–2024, and such pages now almost never hold “korupsi”, so Demokrat's figures after 2015 are slightly too low. “pan” is also the ministry PAN-RB: 0–7%, then 7–10%, then 7–11% of its pages. “pbb” is also the United Nations and a property tax, so Partai Bulan Bintang, PSI and Berkarya are counted by their full names only, which are written less often.
